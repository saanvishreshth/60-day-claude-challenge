# My Claude Usage Strategy

---

## Recommended Primary Model
**Claude Sonnet 4.6**

---

## Why This Model Fits Me

As a student who codes daily and studies/researches a lot, you need a model that's smart enough to handle technical reasoning, explain concepts clearly, and write decent code — without burning through usage limits or feeling slow on every message. Sonnet 4.6 is Anthropic's "everyday workhorse" model: strong at coding, explanations, and general reasoning, while being faster and more efficient than Opus. Since you're a daily user (not occasional), efficiency matters — you'll hit your usage caps much slower on Sonnet than on Opus, while still getting near-Opus quality for 80–90% of student tasks.

Opus becomes your "power tool" for the harder 10–20%: deep research synthesis, complex multi-step coding/debugging, or business strategy documents where reasoning depth really matters.

---

## When to Use Haiku
Use Claude Haiku 4.5 for:
- Quick factual lookups ("What's the syntax for a Python dict comprehension?")
- Summarizing short notes or articles
- Formatting, rewriting, or cleaning up text
- Repetitive small tasks (renaming variables, quick translations, simple edits)
- Anything where speed matters more than depth

**Rule of thumb:** If you'd be happy with an answer from a smart classmate in 5 seconds, use Haiku.

---

## When to Use Sonnet
Use Claude Sonnet 4.6 (your default) for:
- Writing, debugging, and explaining code (most assignments/projects)
- Studying — explanations, practice problems, concept breakdowns
- Drafting essays, reports, notes, or summaries
- General research and comparisons
- Day-to-day back-and-forth conversations

**Rule of thumb:** This is your "default tab" — start here for almost everything.

---

## When to Use Opus
Use Claude Opus 4.6/4.7/4.8 for:
- Complex coding projects (architecture decisions, large refactors, tricky bugs Sonnet struggled with)
- Deep research with many sources/threads to synthesize
- Business strategy documents (business plans, market analysis, pitch decks)
- Long, high-stakes academic writing (thesis chapters, dissertations, research papers)
- Anything where you'll act on the output in a major way (career decisions, financial/business plans)

**Rule of thumb:** If getting it *slightly* better is worth waiting longer and using more of your quota — go Opus.

---

## Recommended Effort Level

### Low
For Haiku-tier tasks: quick lookups, formatting, simple rewrites, short factual questions.

### Standard ✅ (Your default — use most often)
For day-to-day coding help, studying, explanations, drafts, and research summaries on Sonnet. Covers ~70% of your work.

### High
For coding tasks with multiple interacting files/functions, multi-step research questions, or when Standard gave you an answer that felt shallow or missed nuance.

### Max
Reserve for: final-version important documents (business plans, major essays/theses), deep multi-source research projects, or complex debugging where you need Claude to reason through edge cases carefully. Use sparingly — it's slower and more resource-intensive.

---

## Biggest Mistakes I Should Avoid

1. **Using Opus for everything** — wastes your daily quota fast as a heavy daily user; save it for tasks that truly need it.
2. **Using Max effort by default** — slows down responses unnecessarily for simple tasks.
3. **Using Haiku for complex coding/research** — you'll get shallower answers and end up re-asking, wasting time.
4. **Not switching models mid-task** — start coding in Sonnet, but escalate to Opus + High if you're stuck on the same bug after 2–3 tries.
5. **Treating "Deep Research" tasks like quick questions** — give Claude context (what you've already tried, what sources you trust) rather than one-line prompts.
6. **Forgetting to specify effort for important work** — for theses, business plans, or major projects, explicitly bump to High/Max.

---

## Final Recommendation

**If you could use only ONE model and ONE effort level for most of your work:**

> **Claude Sonnet 4.6 + Standard Effort**

**Why:** As a daily-use student balancing coding, learning, research, creative work, and occasional business/strategy tasks, Sonnet at Standard effort gives you the best overall mix of quality, speed, and sustainable usage. It handles the vast majority of your coursework and coding needs comfortably. For the rare big-stakes task (a major project, thesis chapter, or business plan), simply switch to Opus + High/Max for that single task, then come back to Sonnet + Standard for everything else.

Think of it like this: **Sonnet + Standard is your daily backpack — Opus + High/Max is the toolbox you pull out only for the big project.**
