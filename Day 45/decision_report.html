DECISION REPORT
SDE Placement Prep vs. Full-Stack Projects
July 19, 2026
The Real Decision

The real choice isn't "prep vs. projects" — it's whether you optimize for getting hired soon (Option A) or getting genuinely good at building things (Option B).

The trade-off: placement prep buys you resume points and interview readiness now; full-stack projects build the deep skill you said you actually want long-term.

It's hard because both feel urgent, and skipping either feels like falling behind — which is exactly the trap keeping you stuck.

The Case For Each Option
Option A — Placement Prep (DSA, interviews)
Strongest case: It's the fastest, most direct path to landing a job — recruiters filter on DSA rounds first, so skipping this risks not even getting a shot at the roles you want.
Hidden upside: The problem-solving discipline from DSA transfers into writing cleaner, more efficient code in your projects too.
Biggest weakness: It's largely pattern-memorization for interviews — it doesn't teach you how to actually build or ship a product.
Best if you value: Certainty and speed — a job secured on a known timeline.
Option B — Full-Stack Projects
Strongest case: This is the skill you said you actually want to go deep on — and real projects are what separate you in interviews and in the work itself, not just the DSA round.
Hidden upside: A strong project portfolio can get you noticed outside the traditional placement funnel entirely (referrals, internships, freelance).
Biggest weakness: Most placement processes still gate on DSA rounds first — great projects won't save you if you can't clear that filter.
Best if you value: Long-term craft and genuine competence over the fastest job offer.
Assumption Buster

Assumptions you may be making

  • That prep and projects are mutually exclusive — as if you can only ever do one.
  • That "falling behind" is measured against everyone else, rather than against your own actual goal.
  • That going deep on full-stack means abandoning placement chances entirely.

Biases at play

  • FOMO / Social comparison bias — the fear of falling behind peers, not a real gap in your own plan.
  • Status quo / paralysis bias — staying stuck weighing both instead of committing to a primary focus, because committing feels riskier than staying undecided.

What you're definitely ignoring

You already answered this — your gut said B, go deep on one skill. The question isn't really "which one," it's "why haven't you acted on the answer you already gave yourself."

Premortem — 12 Months From Now

If Option A (Placement Prep) failed:

  • You cleared DSA rounds but had nothing substantial to show in the project/system-design part of interviews.
  • You burned out grinding problems with no real product to point to, and felt disconnected from actual engineering.
  • Placements didn't go as planned, and you're left with strong DSA but no deep skill to fall back on.

Early warning sign: You're solving problems but can't explain how you'd build a real feature end-to-end.

Prevention: Build at least one small project in parallel, even 2 hours a week, so you have proof of skill beyond DSA.

If Option B (Full-Stack Projects) failed:

  • You built great projects but couldn't clear the DSA screening round, so you never got the interview to show them.
  • You spent months in "exploration" mode without a clear standard for when a project was "done."
  • Placement season passed and you weren't prepared for the format most companies actually test.

Early warning sign: You're proud of your project but haven't solved a single DSA problem in weeks.

Prevention: Timebox daily DSA practice (even 30-45 min) so the muscle doesn't fully atrophy while you build.

7-Day Test Plan
DAY 1-2
Research: List 3 full-stack project ideas that stretch you, and audit how much DSA you'd realistically need for your target companies.
DAY 3-4
Experiment: Spend focused time building one feature of a chosen project — see how it actually feels to go deep vs. grind problems.
DAY 5-6
Conversation: Talk to a senior/mentor or someone recently placed — ask how they balanced prep and projects, and what they'd do differently.
DAY 7
Decide: If building the project energized you more than the problems did, commit to Option B as primary with a minimum daily DSA floor.
The Verdict
Option B: Full-Stack Projects (Primary)

It wins because it's what your gut already told you, it scores highest on growth and long-term alignment, and it's the one thing you said you actually want to get good at.

This isn't "abandon placement prep" — it's making projects your primary focus, with a small daily floor of DSA so you don't get shut out of interviews.

The hard truth: you don't have a balance problem, you have a commitment problem — you already know what you want, you're just scared to stop hedging.
