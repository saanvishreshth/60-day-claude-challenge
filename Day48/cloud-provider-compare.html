Free tier / student credits
$100 (AWS Educate)
Standard AWS Educate grant is $100 in credit for verified students, on top of the general Free Tier. Some earlier reports cite as low as $35 for individually-verified students without an institutional partnership.
Source: aws.amazon.com/education/awseducate
$300 trial (all users)
Not a student-specific offer — any new customer gets $300 in credit valid for 90 days, plus an Always Free tier on 20+ products with no expiry.
Source: cloud.google.com/free
$100/year (renewable)
Azure for Students gives verified full-time students $100 in credit for 12 months, no credit card required, and can be re-verified annually for as long as you remain a student.
Source: azure.microsoft.com/free/students
Ended Aug 1, 2026
DigitalOcean's $200 GitHub Student Developer Pack credit was retired; the company left the GitHub Student Pack and the offer (including already-issued balances) expired. No dedicated student program remains as of this writing.
Source: DigitalOcean community post; aistudentdiscount.com
Pricing (entry-level compute)
t3.micro ~750 hrs/mo free (12 mo)
EC2 Free Tier covers a small instance for the first 12 months for new accounts, then standard on-demand billing applies.
Source: aws.amazon.com/free
e2-micro always free
One e2-micro VM per month is permanently free in eligible US regions, with no 12-month expiry — useful once trial credit runs out.
Source: cloud.google.com/free
B1s VM ~750 hrs/mo (12 mo)
Azure's free account includes 750 hours of a B1s burstable VM for 12 months, similar structure to AWS's free tier.
Source: azure.microsoft.com/free/students; lifeatlas.site
Droplets from $4/mo, no free tier
DigitalOcean has no ongoing always-free instance — pricing is flat and simple, but a beginner pays from day one once any trial credit is gone.
Source: digitalocean.com pricing (as reported, March 2026)
Certification path & cost
Cloud Practitioner ~$100
Entry-level CLF-C02 costs about $100; associate-level exams (Solutions Architect, Developer) run roughly $150; professional/specialty exams jump to about $300.
Source: whizlabs.com; examcert.app (2026 pricing roundups)
Associate Cloud Engineer ~$125
Foundational Cloud Digital Leader is around $99; the hands-on Associate Cloud Engineer exam is about $125; professional-level exams run roughly $200.
Source: examcert.app; truecert.co (2026 pricing roundups)
AZ-900 ~$99
Fundamentals (AZ-900) is about $99, the cheapest entry-level cloud exam among the three hyperscalers; associate-level role exams (AZ-104, AZ-204) run roughly $165.
Source: whizlabs.com; examcert.app
No vendor certification exists
DigitalOcean does not run a formal certification program comparable to AWS/Azure/GCP; this is a structural gap, not a missing data point.
Based on absence of any DigitalOcean certification listing on digitalocean.com or major cert-tracking sites searched
Learning resources & docs quality
600+ hrs via AWS Educate
AWS Educate bundles 600+ hours of structured courses and labs alongside the credit grant, plus AWS Skill Builder and AWS re:Post community Q&A.
Source: studentdiscounthub.com; aws.amazon.com/education/awseducate
Cloud Skills Boost + free labs
Google Cloud Skills Boost offers guided, hands-on labs often bundled with trial credit; documentation is generally praised for clarity in independent guides.
Source: cloud.google.com; general 2026 guide comparisons (tech-insider.org)
Microsoft Learn (free, structured)
Microsoft Learn is a free, module-based training platform directly tied to certification paths, including a free AZ-900 Cloud Skills Challenge bundled with the student credit signup.
Source: learn.microsoft.com/training/student-hub
Community tutorials (large, informal)
DigitalOcean's Community tutorial library is large and well-regarded for beginner sysadmin topics, but it is not structured into a certification curriculum.
Source: digitalocean.com/community (general reputation, not a specific cited count)
Ease of use for beginners
Widely reported as steepest curve
Multiple 2026 comparison guides describe AWS's console and service catalog (200+ services) as the most complex of the major providers for newcomers; this is a qualitative consensus across sources, not a benchmarked score.
Source: digitalocean.com/resources/articles/comparing-aws-azure-gcp (industry comparison)
Moderate — praised console, fewer services
Independent reviews consistently describe GCP's console as cleaner than AWS's, with a smaller service catalog that's easier to navigate as a beginner.
Source: digitalocean.com/resources/articles/comparing-aws-azure-gcp
Moderate — enterprise-oriented, denser menus
Guides note Azure's portal reflects its enterprise focus with layered pricing and settings that can make costs harder to predict for a first-time user, despite strong free learning content.
Source: digitalocean.com/resources/articles/comparing-aws-azure-gcp
Widely cited as simplest
DigitalOcean's own positioning and third-party reviews consistently describe its "Droplet" model and flat pricing as the most beginner-friendly of the four, deliberately trading depth for simplicity.
Source: gist.github.com DigitalOcean review, 2026

How this was researched ▶

Every data point above came from a live web search conducted July 26, 2026. Official vendor pages (aws.amazon.com, cloud.google.com, azure.microsoft.com, digitalocean.com/docs) were prioritized over third-party blogs; independent roundups (exam-cost aggregators, comparison articles) were used only where the vendor doesn't publish the figure directly, such as certification exam pricing tables and cross-provider comparisons.

Conflicts found and how they were resolved

Azure region count: sources disagreed — one 2026 comparison cited "70+ regions," another cited exactly "75 regions." Both are directionally consistent (Azure has by far the most regions) so the range is reported rather than a single precise number, and the discrepancy is flagged here rather than picking one arbitrarily.
AWS Educate student credit amount: figures ranged from $35 to $100 depending on whether the student's institution has an official partnership with AWS Educate. The $100 figure (institutional partnership) is shown as the headline number since it's the most commonly cited current figure, with the lower bound noted in the detail text.
DigitalOcean student credit: this was a moving target during research — the $200 GitHub Student Pack benefit was live earlier in 2026 but multiple DigitalOcean community threads and a partner site confirm it was retired on August 1, 2026. This is reported as a discontinued offer, not as current data, since presenting it as "$200 available" would be actively wrong by the time a reader acts on it.

What counts as an estimate here

Items flagged estimate are qualitative consensus judgments (e.g. "AWS's console is the steepest learning curve") drawn from how multiple independent comparison articles describe the experience — not a numeric benchmark any vendor publishes. They are included because beginner usability is genuinely part of the decision, but they carry lower confidence than a directly-sourced number like a region count or exam fee.

What the overall ranking is (and isn't)

The 01–04 ranking is an editorial synthesis, built by weighing the sourced facts above toward what a student/certification-prep user cares about most (credit availability, certification cost, and beginner usability, with region count and learning resources as secondary factors). It is not itself a citable statistic — the individual data points beneath it are the actual sourced facts, and are the right thing to check independently before deciding.
